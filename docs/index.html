Texas Transit · a statewide study · not an official plan

What if all of Texas built transit like Dallas–Fort Worth?

Dallas–Fort Worth has a plan to carry two million riders a day by 2100. This asks what the same money per resident would buy across the whole state: $9.0B a year to 2100, for high-speed rail between the cities and rail and bus rapid transit in every metro, all under one budget and valued the same way.

Inside the metros

Every metro gets a network drawn from its own streets

Each metro's lines were drawn by DFW Transit's network generator from Census people and jobs, commute flows and real roads and rail, at DFW's rate per resident, then planned as a whole on the same demand model DFW's plan uses. On DFW itself, given the hand-drawn plan's money, the generator carries as many riders as the plan for each dollar. The big metros each have a full map: line and station pages, a trip planner from any address, and a diagram.

Best ratio first builds whatever returns the most per dollar next, wherever it is. Metros first gives every metro its own stream and lets intercity rail take the rest. The whole map is the cabinet's: high-speed rail built by a delivery office that gets cheaper as it goes, lighter standards on the thin legs, and the branches (Laredo, the Valley, Corpus Christi, the Panhandle, West Texas, Oklahoma City) on money of their own.

The honest part

What would embarrass this plan in a hearing

The wider benefits (congestion, crashes and emissions avoided, and the economic boost of bigger, better-connected cities) are allowances from US and UK practice, not modelled traffic, and they're about a third of the benefits. The table above shows the plan with them halved.

The governor's cabinet has reviewed the plan three times. Its answers, and what every test found, are in the third session and what came of the second.
