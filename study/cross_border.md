**What it leaves out.** Juárez residents' trips within Juárez (Mexico's benefit, not counted); trips south by El Paso residents; growth; the line feeding El Paso's network beyond downtown (the metro model carries crossers from the bridge on); and the inspection itself, whose cost and hours set whether a transit lane can move people faster than the pedestrian queue. The vehicle and pedestrian waits are assumptions, not averages of CBP's published waits. The share of El Paso's jobs a car passenger can reach by transit (57%, within 800 m of a station in the plan) caps how many leave their cars.
