# Houston west: one line or two?

Generated by `python study/hsr_houston_west.py`. `texas/hsr.py` runs two lines west from Houston: US 290 to Austin (Houston - Brenham - Austin) and I-10 to San Antonio (Houston - Columbus - Seguin - San Antonio), next to I-35 south (Austin - San Marcos - San Antonio). In `study/hsr_options.md` I-35 south ranks first, US 290 second and I-10 ninth (+2.8M trips a year for $30.3B once the others exist). This tests whether one combined line serves Houston better per dollar.

**Method.** Each alternative is a set of service patterns: a chain of stations and how often its trains run. Track is every link a pattern uses, costed as `texas/hsr.py` does ($90M per track km, track km = straight-line km between neighbouring stations x 1.12, a major station $600M and a minor one $150M). Demand is `texas/intercity.py` in 2050 with trend growth and induced trips, as in `study/hsr_options.py`; trips a year include induced trips, and travellers' gain is the sum of `benefit_usd` x 365 (consumer surplus, induced trips' own gain left out). Where a line splits, each branch gets half the trunk's trains: the trunk runs every 30 minutes and each branch every 60. The model's one network headway is replaced by the patterns' own (`RailNetwork(..., services=...)`): a trip waits for the trains of every pattern calling at both its stations, half the headway, capped at 30 minutes; a trip no single pattern serves (Dallas - Houston via Austin, say) waits for the least frequent link of its route, with no transfer penalty, as elsewhere in the model. Operating cost is `texas/hsr.py`'s rate per train-km, counted per pattern, so a wye's branches run half the trains. With one headway everywhere, the script reproduces `study/hsr_options.py` for A and S.

**Geography.** From Houston (Texas Central's Northwest Mall site) Austin is 226 km west-northwest and San Antonio 296 km west; they are 118 km apart. The angle between them seen from Houston is small, so a shared trunk can run a long way west before splitting. Junctions are at towns on the roads a line would follow, and are stations ($150M each): Giddings (US 290 trunk via Brenham, 151 km from Houston, the Austin branch carrying on along US 290 and the San Antonio branch heading south-west to Seguin), Flatonia (I-10 trunk via Columbus, 160 km; the Austin branch north-west past Bastrop, the San Antonio branch on along I-10), Columbus (I-10, 105 km; the Austin branch along SH 71 through La Grange and Bastrop, which lie on the straight line) and Lockhart (214 km, via La Grange, which lies on the straight line; 43 km from Austin and 100 from San Antonio via San Marcos, the junction closest to both). B3 alone drops I-35 south: Austin - San Antonio trains run through a triangle junction at Lockhart. C2 reaches Austin from the I-10 line over a 35 km Seguin - San Marcos link and I-35 south. C3 is the Texas Triangle as it is usually drawn on maps: Houston - San Antonio direct and Austin on I-35, with no Houston - Austin line. D is the Texas T-Bone of the Texas High Speed Rail and Transportation Corporation (2000s): an I-35 spine from Dallas - Fort Worth to San Antonio, with Houston's leg joining it near Temple and Killeen via College Station.

### Recommendation

The one line (A) is the best Houston-west configuration on every measure per dollar. On top of I-35 north and Dallas - Shreveport it adds 31.7M trips a year and $1,808M of travellers' gain for $36.7B (0.86M trips and $49M of gain a year per $B), and it stays first built alone, with Texas Central built, and at $60M and $110M per km. Its Houston - San Antonio trains run through Austin in 99 minutes, 16 more than a direct I-10 line, and that difference is worth little: the separate set (S) adds 2.6M trips for $30.3B more (0.09M per $B), as I-10's ninth place in `study/hsr_options.md` already said.

No wye helps. Each wye that keeps I-35 south (B1, B2, C1) costs $16-20B more than A, because the San Antonio branch is new track, and carries fewer trips: splitting the trains halves every Houston service (every 60 minutes, so the wait doubles from 15 to 30 minutes, which outweighs the 6-16 minutes a branch saves on Houston - San Antonio). In B1 Houston - Austin falls from 4.2M trips a year to 3.4M, Dallas - Houston (through Austin) from 0.86M to 0.64M, and even Houston - San Antonio, 6 minutes faster on its branch, carries 2.3M against 2.4M through Austin. Giddings (B1) is the best junction, since it keeps US 290's direct line to Austin. Even with Houston - Austin trains every 30 minutes and San Antonio's every 60, B1 carries 32.0M trips, 0.3M more than A for $17.9B; with both branches every 30 minutes it carries 32.9M, 0.06M per extra $B, below I-10's, at $431M a year more operating cost than A. The cheap combined lines lose riders: the Lockhart wye without I-35 south (B3, $37.6B) slows Austin - San Antonio, the busiest pair, and Houston - Austin; the Texas Triangle as usually drawn (C3) sends Houston - Austin round by San Antonio; reaching Austin from I-10 over a Seguin - San Marcos link (C2) does no better per $B. The T-Bone (D) costs about what A does once I-35 north exists and comes close (0.80M per $B) only because Dallas - Houston rides via Temple; with Texas Central built it falls to 0.62M against A's 0.73M.

For `texas/hsr.py`: no change to its corridors. US 290 + I-35 south already is the one line A (with one headway, the model runs Houston - San Antonio through Austin), and I-10 should stay a separate, late candidate rather than become a wye's San Antonio branch: as an increment over A it is worth more per $B than any branch, and both are near the bottom of the ranking. Replacing US 290 + I-10 with a wye would lower the network's value. The one thing worth noting in `texas/hsr.py` is that US290 and I35S are meant to run as one line, Houston - San Antonio trains through Austin; the demand model and `hsr.opex_musd` already treat them that way.

### Caveats

- There is no transfer penalty, and a trip no single pattern serves waits for the least frequent link of its route. That flatters configurations where big pairs change trains: Houston - Austin at San Antonio in C3 most, and Dallas - Houston at Austin (or Temple) in every alternative alike.
- Each county uses its nearest station. A branch station at Seguin (B1, B2) pulls Guadalupe County off the I-35 south trains onto the hourly branch, which is why Austin - San Antonio carries a little less in B1 than in A; people would choose the better station. That costs B1 under 0.5M trips a year, far less than its gap to A.
- Every link is straight line x 1.12 at $90M per km. A junction's flying junctions and the triangle at Lockhart cost more than plain track, and the Colorado and Guadalupe crossings of the branches are not costed separately; both would only add to the wyes' cost.
- Junction sites are towns on the roads, each a $150M station that also serves its county. Other sites (Elgin, Bastrop, La Grange or Luling as junctions) fall between the ones tested: the geometry is a trade between a long shared trunk and direct branches, and none tested comes near A.
- One speed (260 km/h between stops, 3 minutes a stop) everywhere, and the demand model's parameters are the documented assumptions of `texas/intercity.json`. Rail's constant and induced trips move the totals a lot (A from 0.55M to 1.33M trips per $B) but not the ranking: A is first in every run.
- Operating cost, fares and wider benefits are not netted here; `study/timing.py` values the corridors in `texas/hsr.py` and is unchanged.
