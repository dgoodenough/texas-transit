Line planning (`vendor/dfw-transit/metro/lineplan.py`): after the full model prunes the greedy's pool, a local search on the same model improves the network as a whole: it merges downtown stubs into through lines, adds or swaps lines to reach unserved anchors, drops lines other lines make redundant, swaps a weak line for a better version of its corridor and sets each line's headway, maximizing new daily trips less 2.5 trips a day per $M of cost (capital plus 20 years' operating: the plan's floor, `study/urban_metros.py` PLAN_FLOOR, kept by `study/fixed_point.md`) less a penalty for each unserved anchor. Lines that end near an airport terminal off the road graph get variants with a spur to it (`metro/spurs.py`), lines ending downtown pay for a terminal (`candidates.terminal_cost`), and each line's peak load is checked against its capacity at its headway. Routes between centers also pass anchors no center stands on (a campus beside downtown), and a corridor dense enough for metro where no freeway or major arterial runs gets BRT on its street. The comparison above is against the folder it is run with (here the networks before the final regeneration: floor 2.5, airport spurs, terminal costs, border crossers, the crowding check, metro tunnels only in the densest core). Where a metro's pool makes at most 4,096 networks, every one is then evaluated on the full model and the best kept (the exact program, `lineplan.Planner.exact`). Turn penalties on the line paths (`candidates._search_turns`) were tested and are off: they found no better network.
