# Timing: every layer under the one budget

Generated by `python study/timing.py`. Every project from steps 3-5, valued the same way and scheduled under the statewide budget: $9.0B a year of capital (DFW Transit's rate per resident), of which Dallas-Fort Worth keeps $2.3B for its own plan and $6.7B a year goes to everything here, 2025-2100, in 2025 dollars.

**Value** is what travellers gain, from the same logit models that predict the riders: the logsum gain of every intercity traveller on a pair, and for the urban commute model (where transit's share is small) one money coefficient's worth per new trip, $8. The **ratio** is benefit over cost across 50 years at 3%: travellers' gain (plus any operating surplus) over capital plus the operating deficit (operating cost minus fares). Projects open best ratio first as the budget allows (spending spread over the construction years, carryover allowed, no borrowing). The budget is spent whether or not a ratio reaches 1, since the plan spends DFW's rate; a ratio of 1 or more means travellers' gains alone pay for it.

## Caveats

- One dollar of travellers' gain is treated the same in every layer, but the urban figure is an approximation (exact only as transit's share goes to zero) and counts non-commute trips at the commute value; the intercity figure leaves out induced trips' own gain. Both include each mode's constant, so the ratios are only as good as those constants (high-speed rail's moves its first four corridors between 30M and 74M trips a year).
- An urban trip is worth about $8 to its traveller in DFW Transit's commute model (its cost coefficient, a value of time near $12 an hour), an intercity trip $40-80. So the order favours the Triangle's high-speed lines and most metro lines open after 2050. Valuing urban trips higher, or counting congestion relief, would bring them forward.
- Values grow with Texas's population trend from the year they were estimated; nothing else changes over time (no new land use around stations, no change in car travel or flying).
- Travellers' gain is the only benefit counted: not less congestion for drivers, safety, emissions, or what stations do for land values and the economies of the places they join. Transit projects rarely pass a ratio of 1 on travellers' gains alone in US appraisals; the ratios rank projects more than they judge them.
- Operating cost is outside the capital budget, as in DFW Transit; the deficits are in the ratio, but no budget pays them yet.
- Dallas-Fort Worth's plan runs on its own timetable (DFW Transit) and isn't re-ranked here.
