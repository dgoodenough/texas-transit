# The urban layer: rail and BRT for Texas's other metros

Generated by `python study/urban_metros.py summary` from `urban/generated/<id>/` (DFW Transit's network generator, `vendor/dfw-transit/metro/`, driven from this repo with `METRO_HOME=urban`). Every network is drawn by the generator from Census population and jobs, LODES commutes, today's GTFS and Overture roads and rail; nothing is hand-drawn. Each metro's `report.md` lists its centers, corridors and every line chosen or rejected.

**Budget.** DFW Transit's rate per resident ($2.3B a year for 7.47M people) times the metro's Texas population (2020) over 30 years, the generator's default horizon, in 2025 dollars. Dallas-Fort Worth is not here: it has its own plan, DFW Transit.

**Ridership constant.** The demand model is DFW Transit's commute model. Its constant (how much people favour transit beyond its times and costs) is fitted per metro where NTD counts today's rail or BRT riders, and set from the metro's ACS transit share elsewhere:

| Metro | Today's lines carry (NTD 2024, weekday) | ACS transit share of workers | Constant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dallas-Fort Worth (DFW Transit) | 80,000 rail | 0.67% | +0.430 |
| Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands | 43,920 | 1.51% | +0.875 |

Through DFW and Houston, the constant rises 0.55 for each unit of ln(transit share / DFW's). Every other metro gets that line at its own ACS 2020-2024 share (at least 0.1%); the constants are in `urban/metros.json`.

**Check, Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos:** at its constant (+0.68) the model puts 16,162 riders a day on today's Red Line; NTD counts 1,636. Fitting the constant to that one line would take it to about -2.05, as if Austinites liked transit far less than Dallasites, against ACS. The miss is the line: one train every 30-60 minutes. DFW's calibration is mostly frequent light rail, and the model doesn't penalise infrequent rail enough. Generated regional rail (every 20 minutes) and streetcars (every 20) are likely overrated too, though less: metro (every 6) and BRT (every 8) are where the calibration holds.

**Unspent: $96B of the $239B.** 10 metros get no line at all (Killeen, Longview, Tyler, Abilene, Midland, Sherman, San Angelo, Victoria, Texarkana, Eagle Pass), and most of the other small ones one short BRT line. The generator only drafts lines between activity centers, clusters of at least 20,000 people + jobs at 4,000 per km2 or more, and only gives a corridor a mode when the people + jobs along it reach that mode's density (DFW's calibration, `vendor/dfw-transit/metro/generate.json`). Texas's smaller metros are mostly too spread out for rail or BRT by those tests; their case is for local bus, which this generator doesn't draw. The unspent money goes back to the statewide pot for the timing step.

Metros under 300,000 people (15): $4B for 23,473 new daily trips, 6,693 per $B; the 10 larger ones: 3,268 per $B. The flat per-resident budget is a starting point: the statewide timing step moves money to where it buys the most riders, urban or intercity.

## Caveats

- The model is DFW's commute model with one constant moved per metro. Its time and cost weights, access and parking assumptions are DFW's; the slope rests on one metro, Houston. The Austin check says the model overrates infrequent rail several times over.
- Rapid buses that NTD files as plain bus (MetroRapid, Primo, Brio) are counted as today's frequent buses, not BRT, so the generator may propose BRT with its own lanes on their streets, and Austin's and San Antonio's starting points look emptier than riders see them. Project Connect (Austin) and VIA's Green Line are not in today's network.
- ACS transit shares in college towns (College Station-Bryan, and San Marcos within Austin) include university shuttles; the constant reads them as a liking for transit in general.
- Commutes to and from Mexico (El Paso, Laredo, McAllen, Brownsville, Eagle Pass) are not in LODES.
- The first table's trips are on today's land use; the 2050 and 2100 runs add growth on a trend extrapolation (not an official projection: Austin's counties keep growing fast, to 3.8 times their 2020 population by 2100) and DFW's station-area responses, which assume Texas's other metros zone and build like DFW's plan. Non-commute trips are not in either (DFW Transit's non-commute prototype is off).
