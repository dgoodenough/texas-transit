# Interstate rail: conventional trains to Texas's neighbours and Monterrey

Generated by `python study/interstate_rail.py`. Travel in 2050 (today's long-distance trips grown with each county's population trend, as `study/hsr_options.md`), plus induced trips (5% of the trips rail takes). Corridors and costs: `texas/conventional.py`; service and model: `texas/intercity.json` (`conventional`), `texas/intercity.py`.

Trains run at up to 79 mph on upgraded freight track: 90 km/h between stops, 3 minutes a stop, fares $10 + $0.08 a km, trains spread over 18 hours. Trips under 150 km are left to the car: with two or four trains a day, the wait outweighs the ride.

## Calibration

- **Riders:** the constant against car (-1.74) is set so the Heartland Flyer as it runs today, one train a day each way, carries Amtrak's FY2024 80,000 riders on today's travel.
- **Frequency:** waiting counts 0.075 of the headway (people plan around a timetable), set so four trains a day carry 2.6 times one train's riders, a frequency elasticity of 0.7 (an assumption in the range intercity rail studies find).
- **Monterrey:** a gravity model of trips between US metro zones with a Texas end, 150-1,500 km apart (3743 pairs, R² 0.83): trips rise with (population x population)^1.15 and fall with distance^2.64, times a border factor of 0.3 (assumption; tested at 0.15 and 0.6), with an hour at the border for car and train alike. Monterrey: 5.3M people (INEGI 2020).

  Check: San Antonio - Monterrey comes out at 3,350 trips a day today; San Antonio's airport alone carries 409 passengers a day to and from Monterrey (international segment data, latest 12 months), 12% of that. Houston's and Dallas's airports carry more (about 1,200 and 1,100 a day), but as hubs much of that is connecting; half is counted as local (assumption).

## Caveats

- Only trips with a Texas end are counted (NextGen's Texas extract), so Oklahoma City - Kansas City, Shreveport - Jackson or Little Rock - Memphis riders are missing: the out-of-state corridors are undercounted.
- One calibration point (the Heartland Flyer) and an assumed frequency elasticity. Station-to-station ridership for the Texas Eagle and the Sunset Limited would test it; Amtrak's state fact sheets list station totals.
- Capital is a per-km allowance on top of freight railroads' track, not an engineering estimate: what a host railroad asks for (sidings, double track, dispatching agreements) varies several times over. Kansas City and Memphis need agreements with BNSF and Union Pacific; Monterrey with CPKC and Mexican customs.
- Monterrey's demand is a gravity model with an assumed border factor, not data; the air check is loose.
- These trains would also feed high-speed rail and the urban networks (Fort Worth, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio); the study counts each corridor on its own.
