# High-speed rail options for Texas

Generated by `python study/hsr_options.py`. Travel in 2050: today's trips (NextGen NHTS 2022, air from the airline ticket sample) grown with each zone's population trend (`tools/build_growth.py`: Texas 43M in 2050), plus induced trips (10% of the trips rail takes). Mode choice: `texas/intercity.py`; corridors and costs: `texas/hsr.py` ($90M per track km plus stations, 2025 dollars).

Corridors are added one at a time, each time the one with the most new rail trips a year per $B of its own capital. A trip using several corridors counts once. *Built alone* is the corridor with nothing else: corridors overlap (once Dallas - Austin and Houston - Austin are built, Dallas - Houston trips can already ride through Austin), so a corridor's value depends on what's built before it.

**Caveats.** Every parameter is a documented assumption (`texas/intercity.json`), not fitted to Texas rail data; the constant against car alone moves the first four corridors between 30M and 74M trips a year. Short trips (80-150 km) between metros and nearby rural counties get a few percent rail share, which is generous for a mode with about an hour of access and waiting. Growth is a Census trend extrapolation, not the Texas Demographic Center's projection. Intercity trips are not comparable one for one with the urban layer's daily trips (they are longer and worth more to each traveller); comparing the layers needs user benefits, not trip counts.
