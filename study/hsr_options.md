# High-speed rail options for Texas

Generated by `python study/hsr_options.py`. Travel in 2050: today's trips (NextGen NHTS 2022, air from the airline ticket sample) grown with each zone's population trend (`tools/build_growth.py`: Texas 43M in 2050), plus induced trips (10% of the trips rail takes). Mode choice: `texas/intercity.py`; corridors and costs: `texas/hsr.py` (high unit costs by context: urban approaches, towns, rugged and flat rural ground; track along the drawn alignments; stations; 2025 dollars). Branches wait for their trunks (`texas/hsr.py` REQUIRES). Trains run in service patterns (`texas/hsr.py` PATTERNS: through trains once their corridors are built, else a shuttle on each corridor); a trip no pattern serves changes trains (10 minutes).

Corridors are added one at a time, each time the one with the most new rail trips a year per $B of its own capital. A trip using several corridors counts once. *Built alone* is the corridor with nothing else: corridors overlap (once Dallas - Austin and Houston - Austin are built, Dallas - Houston trips can already ride through Austin), so a corridor's value depends on what's built before it.

### The redrawn network

West Texas branches at Abilene (the Big Country trunk Fort Worth - Weatherford - Abilene, then El Paso and Lubbock - Amarillo legs) instead of separate Fort Worth - El Paso and Fort Worth - Lubbock lines; the Valley is reached from Corpus Christi (Kingsville, Harlingen, Brownsville, a McAllen spur) instead of from San Antonio (`study/cabinet/network.md`). Everything built, same cost model:

| Network | Trips a year | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| Before (Fort Worth - Lubbock, San Antonio - Valley, no Amarillo) | 73.1M | $450B |
| Redrawn (with Amarillo) | 72.9M | $429B |


**Caveats.** Rail's constant is calibrated to Brightline and the Northeast Corridor, falling on trips under 300 km (`study/calibrate_hsr.md`); the other parameters are documented assumptions (`texas/intercity.json`). Growth is a Census trend extrapolation, not the Texas Demographic Center's projection. Intercity trips are not comparable one for one with the urban layer's daily trips (they are longer and worth more to each traveller); comparing the layers needs user benefits, not trip counts.

Operating cost, fares, wider benefits and timing under the budget: `study/timing.md`. Conventional interstate rail and Monterrey: `study/interstate_rail.md`.
