# Calibrating high-speed rail's constant

Generated by `python study/calibrate_hsr.py`. The constant against car (`texas/intercity.json` hsr.asc_vs_car) is how much travellers like a train beyond its time, cost and frequency. It was an assumption (-0.5); the plan's ratios turn on it (`study/cabinet/audit.md`). Here it is fitted, inside the model's own structure, to American markets with fast trains today, with the same national NHTS 2022 data the Texas model uses for car and air, after the Phase 1 fixes (station access counted once; roads 1.1 times the straight line).

| Market | Car trips a day (NHTS 2022) | Air | Rail (target) | Target | Constant: low | central | high |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York - Washington | 34,215 | 5,154 | 12,025 | 65%-75% of air + rail (Amtrak) | +1.28 | +1.50 | +1.74 |
| New York - Boston | 28,751 | 6,533 | 7,367 | 52%-54% of air + rail (Amtrak) | +1.98 | +2.02 | +2.05 |
| Miami - Orlando (Brightline) | 43,215 | 2,877 | 4,110 | 1.3-1.7M riders a year (Brightline 2024) | +0.29 | +0.42 | +0.54 |

**Central constant: +0.42** (Brightline's central fit), **range +0.29 to +2.05**. Before: -0.50, an assumption.

Each market says something different, which is why the plan carries a range, not a point:

- **The Northeast** is a ceiling: rail habits a century old, downtowns where driving and parking are hard, and car-free households the model's car utility can't see (the pivot keeps each pair's car and air split, but not who can drive). Its constant soaks all of that up.
- **Brightline** is a floor: car-dependent Florida, a 3.5-hour trip to an airport station, in its first full year, still ramping up, and a private operator's fares.
- **Texas** is car-dependent like Florida, so the central value is Brightline's, though Texas's lines would be faster and serve downtowns. The range runs to the Northeast's high; the plan reports ratios at both ends (`study/timing.md`).

Caveats: Amtrak's market shares are its own (2010-2014), on its own definitions; the NHTS's car and air counts are modelled from location data; Brightline's 2024 riders are reported by half-year and month, so the year is an estimate; the fit holds the rest of the model (time and cost weights, the nest) at its assumptions.

Sources: [Amtrak air-rail shares (CNBC)](https://www.cnbc.com/id/48690841); [Amtrak 65%/52% (Progressive Railroading)](https://progressiverailroading.com/amtrak/news.aspx?id=24740); [Brightline first-half 2024 (Yahoo/WFTV)](https://www.yahoo.com/news/brightline-record-orlando-miami-ridership-172634417.html); [Brightline 2024 (iHeart)](https://wiod.iheart.com/alternate/amp/2025-05-05-brightline-doubles-ridership-in-2024-despite-losing-549-million-in-2024-despite).
